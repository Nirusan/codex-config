# Wikipedia Signs of AI Writing, adapted for stop-slop

Source used: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing

Use this as an expansion pack for stop-slop, not as a rigid detector. The goal is not to accuse text of being AI-written. The goal is to remove patterns that make prose feel generic, inflated, synthetic, or weirdly over-processed.

## Core principle

AI-ish writing often gets:
- less specific
- more inflated
- more abstract
- more eager to sound important than to say something concrete
- more likely to summarize, frame, and over-explain than to just say the thing

Default fix:
- replace abstraction with specifics
- replace claims of importance with evidence
- replace grand framing with the actual fact
- prefer simple verbs over inflated constructions
- delete fake analysis when no analysis is happening

## Ban these pattern families

### 1. Significance inflation

Cut phrases that try to make ordinary points sound historic, profound, or culturally important.

Examples to avoid:
- a testament to
- serves as a reminder
- underscores the importance of
- highlights the significance of
- plays a vital role
- plays a crucial role
- plays a significant role
- pivotal moment
- key moment
- key turning point
- significant shift
- focal point
- broader movement
- broader trend
- broader landscape
- evolving landscape
- enduring legacy
- lasting impact
- indelible mark
- symbolizing
- deeply rooted
- setting the stage for
- shaping the future of
- reflects a broader
- contributes to the broader
- marking the
- represents a shift
- marks a shift

Default rewrite:
- say what happened
- say who did it
- say what changed
- if the importance is real, prove it with a number, consequence, or example

### 2. Generic ecosystem padding

Ban vague lines about ecosystems, communities, industries, or environments unless they carry concrete information.

Examples to avoid:
- plays a role in the ecosystem
- contributes to the community
- part of a broader effort
- within the broader context of
- important in today’s landscape
- relevant in the current environment
- general health of the ecosystem is crucial
- preserving this is vital not only for

Default rewrite:
- name the concrete effect, or delete it

### 3. Overstated notability and source flexing

Ban writing that tries to prove importance by gesturing at coverage, attention, or credibility instead of making a point.

Examples to avoid:
- featured in leading media outlets
- covered by major publications
- profiled in
- widely recognized as
- notable for
- gained significant attention
- attracted attention from
- has been highlighted by
- independent coverage
- local, regional, or national media outlets
- active social media presence
- maintains a strong digital presence

Default rewrite:
- if the source matters, say what it reported
- if it does not matter, cut it

### 4. Superficial analysis glued onto facts

Ban sentences that attach vague interpretation, praise, or symbolic meaning to plain facts.

Examples to avoid:
- this highlights
- this underscores
- this demonstrates
- this reflects
- this suggests
- this reveals
- emphasizing
- ensuring
- symbolizing
- contributing to
- fostering
- cultivating
- encompassing
- valuable insights
- resonates with
- aligns with

Default rewrite:
- either make the point directly or cut the sentence
- if there is real analysis, make it concrete and attributable

### 5. Abstract praise replacing specifics

Ban language that smooths over the real details and swaps them for vague prestige.

Examples to avoid:
- innovative
- visionary
- groundbreaking
- transformative
- remarkable
- influential
- dynamic
- impactful
- compelling
- vibrant
- rich cultural heritage
- profound
- commitment to
- natural beauty
- diverse array
- renowned

Default rewrite:
- describe the actual behavior, output, or result

### 6. AI-vocabulary clusters

One such word is not enough to matter. Clusters are the tell. When several of these appear together, simplify aggressively.

Words to watch:
- additionally
- align with
- boasts
- bolstered
- crucial
- delve
- emphasizing
- enduring
- enhance
- fostering
- garner
- highlight
- interplay
- intricate
- intricacies
- key
- landscape
- meticulous
- meticulously
- pivotal
- showcase
- showcasing
- tapestry
- testament
- underscore
- valuable
- vibrant

Default rewrite:
- use plainer, more literal words
- if a fancy word is doing no real work, delete it

### 7. Inflated verb choices instead of plain copulatives

AI text often avoids simple is/are/has and replaces them with heavier constructions.

Examples to avoid when a plain verb would do:
- serves as
- stands as
- marks
- represents
- boasts
- features
- maintains
- offers
- holds the distinction of

Default rewrite:
- prefer is, are, has, includes, uses, became, did

### 8. Negative-parallelism framing

Ban faux-clever contrast structures that create drama instead of clarity.

Examples to avoid:
- not just X, but Y
- not only X, but also Y
- it’s not X, it’s Y
- this isn’t X, it’s Y
- no X, no Y, just Z

Default rewrite:
- say the point directly without staging a fake correction

### 9. Rule-of-three padding

AI loves triples because they sound finished even when they add nothing.

Examples to avoid:
- adjective, adjective, adjective
- short phrase, short phrase, and short phrase
- three-item summary lines that say one thing three ways

Default rewrite:
- keep one strong item
- use a list only when each item is genuinely distinct

### 10. Elegant variation for no reason

AI often keeps renaming the same subject to avoid repetition.

Examples to avoid:
- the protagonist, the key player, the figure, the visionary, the builder, all for the same person
- swapping simple repeated nouns for inflated synonyms

Default rewrite:
- repeat the correct noun when needed
- clarity beats variety

### 11. Outline-like challenges and future prospects filler

Ban generic closing sections that follow a school-essay template.

Examples to avoid:
- despite its success, it faces several challenges
- despite these challenges
- future outlook
- future prospects
- challenges and opportunities
- continues to evolve in response to these challenges

Default rewrite:
- name the actual problem
- name the actual next step
- if there is no concrete challenge or future development, cut the section

### 12. Weasel attribution and overgeneralized opinion

Ban vague authority language that implies consensus without naming real people or sources.

Examples to avoid:
- experts argue
- observers have cited
- some critics argue
- industry reports say
- researchers say
- several publications have cited
- such as before a supposedly open-ended list that is actually exhaustive

Default rewrite:
- name the person or source, or cut the claim

### 13. Overformatted listicle energy

AI often slips into deck/listicle formatting instead of prose.

Examples to avoid:
- inline bold label plus colon in every bullet
- key takeaways style bullets
- numbered explainer lists when normal prose would be cleaner
- emoji-led bullets or headings

Default rewrite:
- use simple bullets only when they earn their keep
- avoid turning normal prose into presentation slides

### 14. Excessive boldface and emoji emphasis

Ban formatting that tries to create importance visually.

Examples to avoid:
- bolding lots of key phrases for emphasis
- emoji in section headers or bullets

Default rewrite:
- use plain text emphasis sparingly
- let the sentence carry the weight

### 15. Em dash overuse

AI often uses em dashes to fake rhythm, contrast, or punch.

Default rewrite:
- prefer periods, commas, parentheses, or a rewrite

## Practical stop-slop pass

When editing a text, check for this sequence:

1. Is this sentence more abstract than the real point?
2. Is it trying to sound important instead of being specific?
3. Is it connecting to a broader trend with no proof?
4. Is it praising, summarizing, or framing where it should just state?
5. Is it using inflated verbs where is, has, or does would work?
6. Is it doing fake contrast, fake analysis, or school-essay structure?
7. Can I replace it with a concrete fact, actor, number, or example?

If yes, rewrite or delete.

## Short anti-slop checklist

Before final output:
- no broader-trend claims without evidence
- no importance language without specifics
- no prestige adjectives where a fact should be
- no summary sentence that merely re-announces the previous sentence
- no generic significance inflation
- no fake analysis glue
- no not-X-but-Y contrast unless contrast is actually necessary
- no AI-vocabulary clusters
- no inflated verbs when plain verbs work
- no school-essay challenge or future-outlook filler
